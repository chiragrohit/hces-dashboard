Our calculation — not a raw field in the dataset

How the per-unit percentile numbers were made

  1. Take each household's monthly spending per person: MONTHLY_CONSUMPTION_EXP ÷ HOUSEHOLD_SIZE (raw columns, visit-1 records only).
  2. Sort all households by that value, poorest to richest.
  3. Accumulate the survey weight Multiplier as you go down the list.
  4. At the point where accumulated weight crosses each 1% of the total, read the spending value — that is p1, p2, … p99.
  5. So p50 (the median) is the spending of the middle household: half of all households spend less, half spend more.

The inputs are raw survey data. The percentiles are a standard statistical summary computed from them — no percentile column exists in the source files. Only visit-1 households are used. Small groups are omitted as noise: household filters keep groups with at least 2,000 sampled households, the survey-month filter keeps groups with at least 300, and the state filter shows the 12 richest states/UTs by median. The percentile math itself is the same for every filter.

The Household Consumption Expenditure Survey (HCES) for 2023-24 was conducted by the National Statistical Office (NSO) under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI), Government of India Survey weights normalized to national estimates for display
